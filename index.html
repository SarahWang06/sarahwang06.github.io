( 03 ) A project I care about

“If you can't see the world,
how else can you feel it?”

Where it began

Founded in 2023, Embracing grew out of a question raised at a high school metaverse forum: how can people experience something they cannot see? That question led us to music, and to the idea of bringing disabled and non-disabled musicians together on one stage.

Building Embracing

As co-founder, I lead strategy, partnerships, and sponsorships, working across festival planning, fundraising, and audience engagement. Over four editions, I've helped develop the festival's programming and accessibility features, from vibration vests to live sign language interpretation.

Why it matters

Live music brings people together, but not everyone gets the same chance to be there. China has around 85 million people with disabilities, and most concerts still aren't built with them in mind. Embracing is working to change that, bringing disabled and non-disabled audiences together to feel the music and share moments that belong to everyone.

( 03 ) Accessibility in action

Music you can feel

Every year we ask the same question: who still can't fully join in, and what would change that? These are some of the answers we've tried so far.

01

Vibration vests

We made these with the engineering club at RDFZ. Deaf guests wear them and feel the music as vibration.

02

Sound wall & balloons

Rest a hand on the wall or hold a balloon, and every beat comes through your fingers.

03

Resonance floorboards

Stand or sit on the boards and the low notes travel up through your feet.

04

Live sign language

Our interpreters sign every song and every speech, from the first act to the last.

05

Step-free access

Ramps, accessible restrooms, an accessible shuttle, and a stage every performer can actually get onto.

06

Screen-reader friendly posts

Blind friends told us our posts were hard to follow, so we rewrote them for screen readers and added audio.

MusicOriginal composition · 2023
▶4:26

Infinite Facets

Composer · Performer · Electone · 2023

I wrote this after a summer on the grasslands of Inner Mongolia: riders galloping in robes that billowed like wings, the sound of the horsehead fiddle drifting across the plains, wildflowers everywhere. I'm not Mongolian, but I felt deeply connected to the place and its people.

Inspired by how Queen mixes rock with orchestra, the piece opens on the horsehead fiddle, a traditional Inner Mongolian string instrument with a carved horse head at the top of its neck, like a window into a new world, then slowly lets Western strings grow around it, with Chinese and Western percussion underneath. Cultures sound different, but the need to create and connect is the same.
